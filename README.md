---

## Background & Problem Statement

Cross-year music streaming datasets from public repositories rarely share a consistent schema. Comparing **2023** (`953` tracks × `24` columns) and **2024** (`4,600` tracks × `29` columns) presents several real-world data engineering obstacles:
- **Schema Drift:** Column names differ completely across years (`track_name` vs. `Track`, `streams` vs. `Spotify Streams`, `in_spotify_playlists` vs. `Spotify Playlist Count`), and 2024 introduces extra cross-platform metrics (`TikTok`, `YouTube`, `Soundcloud`, `Pandora`, `TIDAL Popularity` with 100% nulls).
- **Encoding & Formatting Issues:** Both CSVs require `latin-1` encoding, and numeric metrics are stored as comma-formatted strings (`"390,470,936"`).
- **Temporal & Missing Value Differences:** 2023 splits release dates across three integer columns (`released_year`, `released_month`, `released_day`), whereas 2024 uses a single string (`Release Date` e.g. `4/26/2024`) and contains significant missingness (`113` missing `Spotify Streams`, `5` missing `Artist`, `577` missing `Shazam Counts`).

This project builds a reproducible wrangling pipeline to unify both years into a clean master table (`spotify_cleaned.csv`) and conduct multi-platform EDA across Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, and Shazam.

---

## Objectives & Real-World Impact

- **Schema Harmonization:** Align columns, data types, and release `datetime` schemas across two heterogeneous yearly datasets.
- **Statistical Outlier & Skewness Analysis:** Apply `log1p` transformation and IQR boundary detection to characterize extreme viral streaming behavior.
- **Industry Applications:**
  - **Record Label A&R & Promotion:** Track artist catalog depth, multi-platform playlist penetration, and seasonal release patterns.
  - **DSP & Playlist Curators:** Quantify how playlist inclusions on Spotify, Apple Music, and Deezer correlate with total stream volume.
  - **Streaming Economics:** Highlight the "superstar effect" where a small tail of extreme outliers drives billions of cumulative streams.

## Methodology & Pipeline Workflow

```text
spotify-2023.csv (953×24)  ──► Drop audio features, rename, parse numbers, median/conditional impute ──┐
                                                                                                      ├──► pd.concat() ──► Deduplicate ──► IQR Outlier Flag ──► spotify_cleaned.csv ──► EDA
spotify-2024.csv (4600×29) ──► Drop TikTok/YT/etc., parse Release Date, filter >=2011 (V2), impute ───┘
```

### Version Comparison (`V1` vs. `V2`)
1. **`cleaning_EDA_data.ipynb` (Version 1):**
   - Retains all release years (`1930–2024`), imputes missing numerics with overall medians and missing `artist` with `"Unknown"`, merges `953 + 4,600 = 5,553` rows, and removes `2` exact duplicates (`Tennessee Orange`, `Dembow`), yielding **5,551 clean rows × 11 columns**.
2. **`cleaning_EDA_data_version 2.ipynb` (Version 2 — produced `spotify_cleaned.csv`):**
   - Inspects missing-value patterns by release year (`shazam_charts`, `spotify_streams`, etc.).
   - Filters `df_2024` to `released_year >= 2011` (`4,513` rows) and applies year-conditional imputation (`0` for `< 2011`, group median for `>= 2011`).
   - Removes `2` duplicates and flags `shazam_charts` / `spotify_playlist` IQR outliers into `is_outlier`, exporting **4,517 rows × 12 columns**.

### Exploratory Data Analysis Steps (Both Notebooks)
- **Distribution & Skewness:** Raw `spotify_streams` histogram, `np.log1p(spotify_streams)` histogram with KDE, and log-transformed boxplot.
- **Artist & Track Rankings:** Multi-artist splitting (`str.split(', ').explode()`) to rank Top 10/15 artists and tracks across `spotify_playlist`, `apple_playlist`, `deezer_playlist`, `spotify_charts`, `deezer_charts`, `shazam_charts`, and `spotify_streams`.
- **Temporal Release Trends:** Track release distributions grouped by year (`<2011` through `2024`), month (`January–December`), and day of month (`1–31`).
- **Cross-Platform Correlation:** Pearson correlation matrix and Seaborn heatmap across the 7 numerical platform metrics.

## Results & Key Insights

### 1. Summary Statistics (`spotify_cleaned.csv` — 4,517 Tracks)
- **Spotify Streams:** Mean **428.34M**, Median **231.44M**, Min **1,071**, Max **4.28B** (`4,281,468,720`). The wide mean-to-median gap confirms heavy right skewness.
- **Spotify Playlists:** Mean **56,232**, Median **31,199**, Max **590,392** (IQR bounds in V2: `Q1 = 6,647`, `Q3 = 81,489`, identifying `227` extreme playlist outliers; in V1: `Q1 = 3,042.5`, `Q3 = 70,571`, `391` outliers).
- **Release Years:** Peak catalog volume comes from recent releases (`2023`: 1,159 tracks, `2022`: 694 tracks, `2024`: 693 tracks in V2).

### 2. Cross-Platform Correlations

| Metric Pair | V2 (`4,517` rows) | V1 (`5,551` rows) | Interpretation |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| `spotify_playlist` ↔ `spotify_streams` | **0.792** | 0.686 | Playlist inclusion is the strongest linear correlate of Spotify streams |
| `apple_playlist` ↔ `deezer_playlist` | **0.761** | 0.311 | Strong cross-DSP curation overlap on modern tracks (`>=2011`) |
| `apple_playlist` ↔ `spotify_streams` | **0.703** | **0.717** | Cross-platform playlist reach closely tracks streaming volume |
| `spotify_playlist` ↔ `apple_playlist` | **0.693** | 0.553 | Consistent multi-platform playlisting across top hits |
| `shazam_charts` ↔ `spotify_streams` | 0.410 | 0.361 | Moderate link between music discovery and streaming totals |

### 3. Top Artists & Tracks (`spotify_cleaned.csv`)

## Technical Notes & Known Caveats

- **Column Rename Bug in V2 (`cleaning_EDA_data_version 2.ipynb`):** Cell 11 uses `"q1track_name": "track"` instead of `"track_name": "track"`, leaving `track_name` separate from `track` (causing `track_name` to coerce to `float64` with 6 numeric-named tracks like `505.0`, `69.0`, `inf` and `4,511` nulls in `spotify_cleaned.csv`). Use `cleaning_EDA_data.ipynb` (V1) or fix the dictionary key `"q1track_name"` → `"track_name"` in V2 if exact track-name joins across both years are required.
- **Outlier Flag Overwrite:** In both notebooks, `df['is_outlier']` is first computed using `spotify_playlist` bounds and then overwritten in the next cell using `shazam_charts` compared against `spotify_playlist`'s `lower_bound`/`upper_bound`. Computing separate IQR bounds per metric is recommended for future iterations.
